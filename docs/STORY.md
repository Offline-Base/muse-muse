## Alignment paragraph

Muse Muse does the heavy work once in the cloud, and the aim is that what comes out is yours to run offline. Offline Base already makes the other half: Base Drive, an offline AI kit on a flash drive whose setup is designed to work with Wi-Fi and Ethernet off, and a fleet of Jetson Orin Nano boards run from one console, where the model server stays on the board and the fleet agent dials out, so neither listens on the local network. Muse Muse could make what runs there, tuned to one person's job: one-bit Muse Glimmer on a Jetson. For smaller devices, this flow has no Meta chat model planned yet. The fleet has real boards, but this flow has not run on them, and everything it does on a board so far is simulated. A one-bit Glimmer made by hand (the knowledge run's r30b, 5.75 GB) now fits on an 8 GB Jetson and answers short chats, at 5 to 6 tokens a second with weak answers. Under a 40-receipt bench it gave no valid JSON and the board hung partway (PR #62). Meta's smallest published Glimmer file is 16.8 GB. See `/offline`.

## Privacy paragraph

The owner lifted the no-company-name rule for Muse Privacy content on Nov 1. `components/story/__tests__/story.test.tsx` and the `/private` test still enforce it, so change those tests first if this paragraph should name the company.

Muse can reach your files through connectors, but what it reads lands in its cloud computer, names and all, so private work like grading stays out of its reach. Muse Privacy is a connector you run yourself. Muse plans with tags like [Student A], Muse Glimmer 30B does every step that touches the documents, your policy file decides what can leave, and a final check runs on every result. For the submission, Glimmer runs in the cloud through OpenRouter, on invented data only; Glimmer on your own device is the target, not a result. It is aimed at a hostile or compromised Muse and at instructions planted in documents. Numbers on today's setup, on Glimmer through OpenRouter (Nov 2, `privacy/eval/reports/glimmer_fresh_200_b_2026-11-02.md`): 0 leaks of names or identifiers on 200 invented items, nothing shared that should have waited or stayed, the router right 71.5% of the time (143 of 200), and 101 approval requests, 50 of them needless; 75.0% (15 of 20) on the holdout items, with 0 leaks. An earlier version on Glimmer through OpenRouter (Oct 27, `privacy/eval/reports/final.md`) had 0 leaks of names or identifiers on 100 invented items, with the router right 76% of the time. **Not proven for the submission,** because they were measured with Liquid's LFM3-VL-2B, which isn't a Meta model: 0 leaks on 300 items, 10 leaks on 200 items on Oct 30 (`privacy/eval/reports/device_only_fresh_200.md`), 82.0% agreement on 200 items and 81.0% on 100 tasks, 65.0% (13 of 20) on the holdout items inside those tasks, 5 results shared that should have waited, and 76 approval requests on the 200 items, 28 needless. "Leaks" doesn't cover everything: a summary can point at someone without naming them. There are no real users, the sealed test set hasn't been scored, and the attacks are hand-written. It sits beside Muse Muse rather than inside it: you build with made-up data in Muse Muse, and Muse Privacy is how Muse can help when the real files must stay with you. See `/private`.
