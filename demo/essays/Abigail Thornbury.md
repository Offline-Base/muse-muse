# Abigail Thornbury: Should our school have a garden? Explain your opinion.

Yes, our school should have a garden, and I have three reasons why.

First, a garden would help us learn science in a way we can actually touch. In class we read about how seeds sprout and how plants need sunlight and water, but in a garden we could watch it happen every day. We could measure how tall the bean plants grow each week and make a graph, which is math and science together.

Second, a garden would give our school healthy food. My grandma has a garden, and when I help her pick tomatoes and snap peas, I eat way more vegetables than I do at home. If kids grow their own carrots, I think they would want to try them. Our cafeteria could even use some of the lettuce for the salad bar.

Third, a garden would make our school prettier and calmer. Right now the space behind the gym is just dirt and an old bench. With flowers and a few raised beds, it would be a nice place to read or think. My friend Keturah and I already planned where the sunflowers could go so they would not block the other plants.

Some people might say a garden is too much work. That is true, but every class could take turns, and we could make a watering chart so nobody has to do it all.

In conclusion, a garden would help us learn, eat better, and enjoy our school more. I hope we start one this spring.
