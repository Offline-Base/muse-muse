# Keturah Mensah: Should our school have a garden? Explain your opinion.

Imagine walking out of our classroom and seeing rows of bright green beans, purple eggplants, and yellow squash. I believe our school should have a garden because it would make learning more exciting, teach us to care for living things, and make our school a happier place.

First, a garden would make learning exciting. When we studied plant parts, half the class mixed up the stem and the roots. If we pulled up a carrot in the garden, nobody would ever forget which part is the root again! My friend Abby says she learns best when she can see things, and I think a lot of kids are like that.

Second, a garden teaches responsibility. Plants cannot tell you when they are thirsty. You have to check the soil, notice when the leaves droop, and remember to come back. In Ghana, my grandmother grows okra and peppers, and she says a garden teaches patience because you cannot rush a plant.

Third, a garden would make our school happier. Scientists have found that spending time around plants can help people feel calmer. Our playground is mostly blacktop, and a garden would add color and shade.

Some people may argue that students will lose interest after a few weeks. To solve this, each class could adopt one bed and choose what to plant, so everyone feels proud of their own part.

In conclusion, a school garden would help us learn, grow, and feel good. Let's dig in!
