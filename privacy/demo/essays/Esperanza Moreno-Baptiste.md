# Esperanza Moreno-Baptiste: Should our school have a garden? Explain your opinion.

Should our school have a garden? I strongly believe the answer is yes, because a garden can teach us, feed us, and bring people together.

To begin with, a garden is like an outdoor classroom. We just finished our unit on life cycles, and I noticed that a lot of my classmates understood it better when we watched the butterfly video than when we read the chapter. A garden would let us see life cycles in real life, from seed to sprout to flower to seed again.

In addition, a garden can feed people. My abuela grows peppers, tomatoes, and calabaza in her backyard on Fennel Street, and every August she gives bags of vegetables to the neighbors. She says a small garden can make more food than one family can eat. If our school had a garden, we could donate extra food to the food pantry.

Finally, a garden brings people together. When my abuela works in her garden, neighbors stop to talk to her over the fence. At school, families could come help on Saturdays, and kids from different classes could work side by side.

Some people worry that the garden will be ruined over summer break. One solution is to plant things that are ready by June, like lettuce and radishes, and ask families to sign up for summer watering weeks.

For all of these reasons, our school should definitely have a garden. It would make our school a better place to learn and grow.
