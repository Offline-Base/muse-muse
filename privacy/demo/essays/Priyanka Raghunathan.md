# Priyanka Raghunathan: Should our school have a garden? Explain your opinion.

Every day, our class throws away a full bag of lunch scraps. What if those scraps could turn into soil, and that soil could grow food for our school? That is exactly why I believe our school needs a garden.

My first reason is that a garden turns waste into something useful. When food scraps go to a landfill, they rot without air and give off a gas that warms the planet. In a compost bin, the same scraps become rich soil in about three months. Our class could weigh our scraps each week and track how much we save from the trash.

My second reason is that a garden helps students learn. A garden connects to almost every subject. In math, we could calculate the area of each bed. In science, we could test whether plants grow faster with compost. In writing, we could keep observation journals, just like real scientists do.

My third reason is that a garden builds community. My grandparents in Chennai grow curry leaves and tomatoes on their roof, and the whole building shares the harvest. My cousins call me Pinky because I always wear pink garden gloves when I help them. A school garden could bring families, teachers, and students together in the same way.

Some people argue that a garden is expensive. However, many garden supplies can be donated, and a compost bin can be built from old pallets for almost nothing.

In conclusion, a school garden would reduce waste, strengthen learning, and bring our community closer. Let's turn our lunch scraps into something amazing.
